    What changes when a JPG becomes a PNG

    Every time a JPG is opened, edited and saved again, the JPEG encoder throws away a little more detail, and the artefacts build up: smeared edges, blocky skies, halos around text. Converting once to PNG freezes the image in its current state, so later edits cost nothing more.

    The trade-off is size. JPG was designed for photographs and PNG was not, so expect a much larger file. If an upload form or website asks for PNG, this is the way to meet it; if it accepts either, sending the original JPG is usually better.
