How business days are counted

A business day, or working day, is any day that is neither a weekend day nor a holiday. To count them between two dates, the calculator takes every calendar day in the range, removes the weekend days you chose, then removes the listed holidays that fall on the remaining days. What is left is the number of business days. The three figures under the result show how many calendar days were looked at and how many of them were skipped as weekend or holiday.

Whether the first and last day belong to the range matters. Counting Monday to Friday of one week with both ends included gives 5, and with the start date left out it gives 4. The defaults match the spreadsheet function NETWORKDAYS, which counts both ends.

Adding business days works the other way round, like WORKDAY. The tool starts the day after the start date and steps forward, or back when you subtract, counting only days that are not weekend days or holidays, until it has counted the number you asked for. The start date itself is never counted, so adding 0 days returns the start date, even if that date is a weekend.

The weekend is a setting because it is not the same everywhere. Many countries use Saturday and Sunday, several in the Middle East use Friday and Saturday, and some workplaces work six days. Holidays are yours to supply. Shifted or substitute holidays are not added automatically, so list the day that is actually observed.
