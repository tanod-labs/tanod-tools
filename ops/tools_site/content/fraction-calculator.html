Steps

    Why fractions are worth keeping exact

    A decimal such as 0.333333 only approximates one third, and every operation on approximations adds a little more error. A fraction is exact: 1/3 + 1/6 is precisely 1/2. This calculator therefore keeps every value as a pair of whole numbers, numerator and denominator, and reduces the pair by dividing both by their greatest common divisor after each step. Only at the end does it work out the decimal, by long division, and say which digits repeat.

    Adding and subtracting needs a shared denominator. The least common denominator is the smallest number that both denominators divide into: for 1/4 and 1/6 it is 12, so the sum is 3/12 + 2/12 = 5/12. Multiplying is more direct, numerator times numerator over denominator times denominator. Dividing turns into multiplying by the reciprocal of the second fraction, which is why you cannot divide by a fraction equal to zero.

    A mixed number such as 3 1/7 is just a whole number plus a proper fraction, and it equals the improper fraction 22/7. Converting back and forth is a matter of multiplying the whole part by the denominator and adding the numerator. Negative values keep their sign on the whole: −3 1/7 means −(3 + 1/7), the same as −22/7.
