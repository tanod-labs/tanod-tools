How the harmonies are built

A colour harmony is a set of colours whose hues sit at fixed angles around the colour wheel. Analogous colours are neighbours, 30 degrees apart. A complementary colour is 180 degrees away, a split-complementary pair sits 150 and 210 degrees away, a triad is spaced 120 degrees apart, and a tetrad uses four hues 90 degrees apart. The monochromatic palette keeps the hue and steps the lightness from pale tints to dark shades.

The wheel used here is OKLCH, the cylindrical form of Björn Ottosson's OKLab space. The conversion goes from sRGB to linear light, then through the OKLab matrices, and back again with a gamut check. If a rotated colour falls outside what sRGB can show, its chroma is reduced in small steps until it fits, so the lightness and hue you chose are kept.

Contrast is reported for every swatch against both white and black, because the first question about a palette colour is usually what text can sit on it. The ratio and the AA or AAA wording are always written out, so the information does not depend on seeing colour.
