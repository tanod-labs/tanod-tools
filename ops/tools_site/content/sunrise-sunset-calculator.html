How the times are calculated

The Sun's position uses the low-accuracy formulas of Jean Meeus, Astronomical Algorithms (2nd ed., Willmann-Bell, 1998), chapter 25, and the Moon's position uses the 33 largest longitude and distance terms and 14 largest latitude terms of chapter 47. Sidereal time follows chapter 12 and the lunar phase angle chapter 48. Altitude and azimuth come from the hour angle and declination, with a parallax correction for the Moon.

The page samples the altitude every ten minutes across the local day and bisects each crossing to a fraction of a second. Sunrise and sunset use a Sun-centre altitude of −0.833° (34′ refraction plus 16′ semi-diameter); the twilights use −6°, −12° and −18°; moonrise and moonset use the upper limb with the same refraction, allowing for the Moon's parallax and apparent size. Solar noon is the time of the Sun's highest altitude. The code is a port of the one behind our sun and moon API, which is tested against the JPL DE421 ephemeris.

Results assume a flat horizon at sea level and standard atmosphere; mountains, high ground and weather change the observed times by minutes. Not for navigation, flight planning or legal sunrise and sunset times.
