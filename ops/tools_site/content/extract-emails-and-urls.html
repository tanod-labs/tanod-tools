How the lists are built

The tool scans your text with patterns for each kind of item and shows the matches in the order they appear. An email address is a name, an @ sign and a domain with a valid ending. A URL starts with http, https, ftp or www and runs to the next space, quote or angle bracket, and the punctuation of the sentence around it (a full stop, a comma, a closing bracket without an opening partner) is not counted as part of the address. A domain is the host name of a URL or the part of an email address after the @, with any leading www. removed.

IPv4 addresses are four numbers from 0 to 255 separated by dots, so 192.168.1.1 is listed and 999.1.1.1 is not. Phone-like numbers are found by shape, because a phone number can be written in many ways: 7 to 15 digits, with spaces, dashes, dots or brackets, and an optional plus sign. Dates written as 2026-10-08 are not mistaken for phone numbers. Hashtags start with a # and need at least one letter, so #2026 is skipped.

With duplicates removed, each list shows every distinct item once with the number of times it appeared, which is handy for finding the most frequent sender or the most linked site. You can sort a list A to Z or by count, then copy it with one item per line, or download it as a plain text file or as a CSV with a value and a count column.

Tips

  • To tidy a list you copied, remove repeated lines with Remove duplicate lines or put it in order with Sort lines alphabetically.
  • Need a pattern the tool does not cover? Build one in the regex tester.
  • Links with percent signs in them can be read with URL encode and decode.
