Lap times, split times and how a browser stopwatch measures them

A stopwatch records two numbers every time you press Lap. The split is the whole time since you started. The lap time is the split minus the previous split. Runners and swimmers read lap times to see whether they are fading; people timing a process, such as steps in a recipe or rounds in a meeting, usually want the splits. Showing both lets you check one against the other: the lap times add up to the latest split.

The page records the browser's monotonic clock when you press Start and again whenever it redraws, and shows the difference. It never adds up "ticks", which is why the number stays right when the browser slows down a background tab, and why pausing and resuming does not lose time: the time before a stop is stored and the new run adds to it. The monotonic clock cannot jump when your computer adjusts its time or changes daylight-saving time.

Lap times are kept in hundredths of a second as whole numbers. That avoids the rounding trap where the rounded laps of 1.234 and 2.345 seconds add up to something different from the rounded total, so what you see always sums.
