What each filter does to the pixels

Every pixel is three numbers from 0 to 255, for red, green and blue. Brightness multiplies those numbers, so 150 per cent turns a value of 100 into 150 and anything past 255 is clipped to white. Contrast stretches the numbers away from mid-grey (128) or squeezes them towards it, which is why 0 per cent contrast gives a flat grey picture. Saturation and hue rotation mix the three channels using fixed colour matrices, which is how a photo drifts towards grey or around the colour wheel.

Grayscale replaces each pixel with its luminance, a weighted sum that counts green most (about 72 per cent), red next (21 per cent) and blue least (7 per cent), because the eye is most sensitive to green. Sepia uses a matrix that gives the warm brown tint of old prints. Invert subtracts every value from 255 to make a negative. Blur averages each pixel with its neighbours, three times in each direction, which closely approximates a Gaussian blur.

Tips

  • To read an exact colour from the result, use the Image color picker.
  • Filtering before adding a caption keeps the text crisp: do it in that order with Add text to image.
  • A blurred, high-contrast image compresses very differently, so check the size afterwards in Compress image.
