    What HEIC is, and how this tool reads it

    HEIC is the picture format iPhones have saved photos in since iOS 11, and many Android phones use it too. It stores a photo in about half the space of a JPG at a similar look, by using the same compression as modern video, called HEVC. The price is compatibility: older Windows versions, many web forms, print shops and photo editors still can't open it, which is why you end up needing a JPG.

    Making a JPG from a HEIC takes two steps: decode the HEVC picture, then encode it again as JPEG. Your browser can do the second step by itself, but most browsers can't do the first. So this page carries a small decoder written in JavaScript, the open-source ts-heic library. It reads the file's container, decodes the picture (phones store big photos as a grid of tiles, which it decodes one by one and joins), turns the result upright, and hands the pixels to your browser's own JPEG encoder. It runs in a background worker, so the page stays responsive while it works.

    A JPG is usually bigger than the HEIC it came from, because JPEG compresses less efficiently, and converting is one way: keep the HEIC if you want the smallest file and the best quality. The decoder reads the common kind of HEIC photo and tells you when a file uses something else, such as 10-bit colour or lossless coding, rather than saving a broken picture.

    Tips

    • To make the JPGs smaller for email or a form, run them through Compress image or shrink them with Resize image.
    • Need another format? Convert image handles PNG, JPEG and WebP, and Image to WebP makes small web images.
    • The JPG carries no camera or location data. To clean other pictures the same way, use Remove EXIF.
