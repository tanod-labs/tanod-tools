    Per Connection Queue in practice

    PCQ is a queue type, defined under /queue type with kind=pcq, that a simple queue or a queue tree then uses. Its pcq-classifier decides what counts as one stream: dst-address for downloads, so each client's download is one stream, and src-address for uploads. RouterOS creates the sub-streams on the fly and removes them when they go quiet, so there is nothing to maintain as clients come and go.

    Two settings shape the result. pcq-rate caps each stream; leave it at 0 and the streams simply share whatever the queue's max-limit allows, equally. The max-limit of the queue that uses PCQ should be a little under the real capacity of the link: PCQ can only share bandwidth fairly when packets queue on the router rather than in the modem upstream.

    Simple queue or queue tree?

    The simple queue method needs no marking: a single queue with the LAN subnet as target and queue=upload-type/download-type. The queue tree method uses mangle to mark connections from LAN to WAN and two queues under parent=global; it leaves traffic between local networks unshaped and is the starting point for priority classes. Either way, burst settings per client belong in simple queues; compare them with the simple queue generator and the burst calculator.
