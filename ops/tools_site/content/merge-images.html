    How pictures of different sizes are joined

    A row of pictures is as tall as its tallest member, and a column is as wide as its widest. Anything smaller leaves spare space, so the alignment setting decides where the picture sits in it: at the start, the centre or the end. The spare space is filled with your background colour, or left see-through in PNG and WebP.

    Scaling is the other way to deal with mismatched sizes. Scaling to the smallest picture never enlarges anything, so nothing gets blurry, but large photos lose detail. Scaling to the largest keeps the big ones sharp and stretches the small ones. In a grid the same idea applies to a single cell shape that every picture is fitted into.

    Widths and heights add up fast. Five 4000-pixel-wide photos in a row make a 20,000-pixel-wide image, which may be more than a browser can handle, so the page stops you and tells you the size before you press the button.

    Tips

    • For a printable or shareable collage, add a frame afterwards with Add border to image.
    • If the merged file is too heavy to send, Compress image or Resize image will shrink it.
    • Want the pictures in a document instead? JPG to PDF puts each one on its own page.
