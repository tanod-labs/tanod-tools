Readability scores need at least 100 words of English text. They are approximate: syllables are estimated with a simple rule set.

How this word counter works

The counts are recalculated in your browser each time the text changes, so there is no button to press and nothing leaves your device. Words are found by looking for runs of letters and digits; for languages that don't put spaces between words, the browser's own word-boundary rules are used instead, which is why a Chinese or Japanese sentence counts as several words rather than one.

Keyword density shows how often each word or two- and three-word phrase appears, as a share of all the words in the text. Switching on "Skip common English words" hides words such as "the" and "and" so the topic words stand out. Phrases are listed only if they appear at least twice.

Word limits are usually set by whoever receives the text, and each system counts a little differently. If a limit is tight, leave yourself a small margin.

Tips

  • Tidy a draft before counting with Remove duplicate lines or Find and replace.
  • Changed a draft and want to see what moved? Use Compare text.
  • Fix capitalisation with the Case converter.
  • Checking a post, text message or meta description against a character limit? The Character counter shows what is left.
