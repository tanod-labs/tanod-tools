In words

How numbers are turned into words

The number is split into groups of three digits from the right, and each group is written as hundreds, tens and ones followed by its scale word: thousand, million, billion, trillion and on up to decillion. Tens from twenty to ninety are joined to the ones with a hyphen (forty-two). Because numbers are held as exact whole numbers rather than floating-point values, a number with 15 or 30 digits comes out digit for digit with no rounding.

An ordinal changes only the last word: one becomes first, two second, three third, five fifth, eight eighth, nine ninth and twelve twelfth, and every other number takes th, with a y turning into ieth (twenty becomes twentieth). So 121 is one hundred twenty-first, and 1,000,000 is one millionth.

An amount in words writes the whole part with the currency name in the singular or plural form, then the minor unit: one dollar, two dollars and fifty cents, one pound and one penny, two pounds and five pence. Cheques and invoices often write the cents as a fraction instead, as in One thousand two hundred thirty-four dollars and 56/100, and often use capital letters for every word, both of which you can choose. Currencies such as the Japanese yen have no minor unit, and the Kuwaiti dinar has three decimals.
