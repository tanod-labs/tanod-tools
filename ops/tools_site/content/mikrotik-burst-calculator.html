    Burst in RouterOS queues, step by step

    Burst lets a client go faster than its max-limit for a short time, so web pages and small downloads feel quick while long transfers still settle at the plan's rate. Four settings control it, each given as upload/download in a simple queue: max-limit, the normal ceiling; burst-limit, the higher ceiling while bursting; burst-threshold, the average rate at which bursting stops; and burst-time, the window over which that average is measured.

    RouterOS splits burst-time into 16 slots. After each slot it averages the client's rate over the last 16 slots and compares it with the threshold: below it, the client may use burst-limit; at or above it, only max-limit. A client coming from idle starts with an average of zero, sends at burst-limit, and the average climbs until it crosses the threshold. With 8 s burst-time, a 40M burst-limit and a 16M threshold, the average reaches the threshold after 3.2 s, and the next check, at 3.5 s, ends the burst; the client has moved about 17.5 MB by then. Then the average stays above the threshold as long as the client keeps transferring at max-limit, so the next burst needs a pause.

    Choosing values

    • Set burst-limit to the speed you want short transfers to feel like, often 1.5 to 2 times max-limit.
    • Keep burst-threshold below max-limit, typically 60 to 80% of it, and tune burst-time to get the burst length you want.
    • Turn FastTrack off, or exclude queued clients from it: fasttracked packets skip simple queues entirely. Generate the queues with the simple queue generator or as PPP profiles with the PPPoE server generator.
