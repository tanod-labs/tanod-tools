One or two digits is a runway number, three digits a heading in degrees.

Wind components for each end of the runway
Runway endWindHeadwind or tailwindCrosswind

The formula

Let θ be the angle from the runway heading to the direction the wind is blowing from, and V the wind speed. The component along the runway is headwind = V · cos θ (negative means tailwind) and the component across it is crosswind = V · sin θ, positive from the right and negative from the left. This is the standard wind-triangle decomposition used in pilot training, for example in the FAA Pilot's Handbook of Aeronautical Knowledge (FAA-H-8083-25, chapter on aircraft performance) and the Airplane Flying Handbook (FAA-H-8083-3).

The opposite runway end has a heading 180 degrees away, so its headwind is the negative of the first and its crosswind switches side. The runway number is the magnetic heading in tens of degrees rounded to the nearest ten. Speeds are converted exactly (1 knot = 1.852 km/h, 1 mph = 1.609344 km/h, 1 m/s = 3.6 km/h).

The calculation ignores wind shear, gust fronts and terrain effects, and uses a single wind value. Not for flight planning or navigation.
