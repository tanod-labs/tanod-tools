How word frequency is counted

The text is split into words, each word is lowercased, and the tool counts how many times each one appears. The ranked list puts the most frequent first and breaks ties alphabetically. Counting how often words repeat is a quick way to see what a text is really about, to check whether a keyword is overused, to find the terms in an interview or a set of survey answers, or to spot a habit such as leaning on the same word in an essay.

Without any filtering, the top of the list is nearly always function words such as the, of and and. Choosing a stop-word language removes them, so the content words rise to the top. The lists cover the most common articles, pronouns, prepositions, conjunctions and auxiliary verbs of the language and are meant for a quick look, not for linguistic work.

Phrases are counted as runs of two or three words next to each other, inside one sentence. Repeated phrases are often more telling than single words: they reveal the product names, slogans and set expressions in a text. With stop words switched on, phrases that begin or end with a stop word are dropped, so what remains are more meaningful.

The percentage of an item is its count divided by the total counted. That total changes with the options, because stop words and numbers that are left out are not counted. Download the CSV to keep the full list or to chart it in a spreadsheet.
