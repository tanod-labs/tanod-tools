Read a METAR or TAF time group

How the conversion works

UTC (Coordinated Universal Time) is the international civil time standard maintained by the BIPM and defined in ITU-R Recommendation TF.460. It is written with the suffix Z in aviation, from the military time zone letter Zulu, and in the ICAO time group format DDHHMMZ used by METAR, TAF and SIGMET messages (ICAO Annex 3, WMO No. 306). Local time is UTC plus the zone's offset, which depends on the date because of daylight saving rules.

The page turns the date and clock time you enter into an instant using the offset the zone had at that moment, and then shows the instant in the other zone. This uses the IANA Time Zone Database through your browser's Intl API, the same data used by operating systems. An invalid local time in the hour skipped at the start of daylight saving is moved forward by the gap, and an ambiguous time in the repeated hour uses the first occurrence.

A time group has no month or year, so the tool tries the previous, the same and the next month and keeps the date nearest to the reference date you give (today, UTC, by default), which is how a weather report is normally matched to a calendar. Not for flight planning or navigation.
