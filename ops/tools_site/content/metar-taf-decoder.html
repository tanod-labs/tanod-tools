One report, or several separated by = or by lines starting with METAR, SPECI or TAF.

How the decoder reads a report

A METAR or TAF is a string of space-separated groups in a fixed order, defined by ICAO Annex 3 and WMO Manual on Codes No. 306 (FM 15 METAR and FM 51 TAF) and, for the United States, by the FAA Aeronautical Information Manual 7-1-30. The decoder reads the station code, the day and time in UTC (DDHHMMZ), the wind (direction, speed, gusts and variation), the visibility in metres or statute miles, runway visual range, present weather, cloud layers, vertical visibility, temperature and dew point, and the altimeter setting (Q is hPa, A is inHg in hundredths).

Weather codes combine an intensity (- light, + heavy, VC in the vicinity), descriptors (TS, SH, FZ and others) and phenomena (RA rain, SN snow, FG fog, BR mist and so on). Cloud groups give the amount (FEW, SCT, BKN, OVC) and the base in hundreds of feet above ground level, with CB or TCU for convective cloud. Relative humidity is estimated from the temperature and dew point with the Magnus formula, es = 6.1094 e^(17.625 T / (T + 243.04)), so it is approximate.

Flight categories follow the FAA definitions: the ceiling is the lowest broken or overcast layer or vertical visibility, and the category is set by the worse of ceiling and visibility. For a TAF, each base or change group is decoded on its own, with its time range in UTC and its own category. Times are given as a day of the month and a UTC time because the reports do not carry a month or year.

Not for flight planning or navigation. This is an educational reader. Always get weather from an official source before flying.
