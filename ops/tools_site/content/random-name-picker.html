    Picking fairly, and why the wheel is only for show

    A fair draw gives every entry the same chance. The usual trap is to spin an animation, read off where it stops and call that the result: the physics of a drawn wheel, a slow frame or a rounding error can then favour some slices. Here the order is reversed. The winning entry is chosen first by drawing a random index with rejection sampling, which throws away the uneven remainder that a plain modulo would leave, and the wheel is then rotated to end on that slice.

    Picking several winners works the same way without replacement. The page runs a partial Fisher-Yates shuffle over the positions in your list and takes the first few, so a position can never be chosen twice in one draw. Teams use the full shuffle, then deal names out one by one around the teams. That guarantees sizes differ by at most one, while which people end up together stays random.

    For anything with stakes, such as a giveaway, keep the list visible when you draw and say how it was done. Reading the history aloud or posting it afterwards is a better audit trail than a screenshot of a wheel.

    Tips

    • Tidy a pasted list first: remove duplicate lines if every person should have one entry only.
    • Need numbers, not names, such as ticket 1 to 500? Use the random number generator.
    • Running a quiz round or a talk? Give each turn a limit with the countdown timer.
