Making text readable on a photo

Text on a picture has to stand out from whatever is behind it, and photos rarely give you a plain background. Three tools help. An outline in a dark colour keeps white letters legible over bright areas. A soft shadow lifts the text slightly without a hard edge. A semi-transparent box behind the lines gives the steadiest contrast, at the cost of covering part of the picture.

Because the size is a percentage of the image width, the layout you see in the preview is the layout you get in the file, however large the original is. The padding, outline thickness and shadow also scale with the text, so nothing looks heavier at full size than it did on screen.

Put captions in the lower third where viewers expect them, keep lines short, and leave a margin from the edge; the nine-point grid keeps a small margin automatically. Centre-aligned text suits titles, left-aligned text suits several lines of captions.

Tips

  • Adjust brightness or contrast first with Image filters if the picture is too busy for text.
  • Frame the finished picture with Add border to image.
