    How hidden characters are found

    Unicode defines many characters that print nothing: zero-width spaces and joiners, marks that flip the direction of text, soft hyphens, spaces of unusual widths and the tag characters that mirror ASCII. They are useful in the right place and a nuisance or a weapon in the wrong one. This tool reads your text one code point at a time and sorts each hit into a kind, so you can see what is there before you decide what to do about it.

    Seven kinds are reported. Zero-width characters include the zero width space, the joiners and the byte order mark. Text-direction controls are the embeddings, overrides, isolates and marks used in Trojan Source attacks on source code. Unicode tag characters are the U+E0000 block, used to smuggle invisible instructions into text for AI assistants; each run is decoded so you can read the ASCII it spells. Control characters are the C0 and C1 codes other than tab and line breaks. Other invisible characters cover the soft hyphen, invisible math operators, fillers and variation selectors, and unusual spaces are the no-break space, the em and en spaces and the ideographic space. Finally, mixed-script words are words that combine Latin letters with Cyrillic, Greek or Armenian ones, the usual way of faking a domain name or a username.

    Zero-width spaces turn up in AI-generated text too. Chat assistants and text pipelines sometimes leave zero-width spaces or joiners in their output, and the same characters can be used as an invisible watermark, so a pasted answer may carry more than you can see. Running the text through this check shows exactly what is hidden in it.

    Some of these characters belong in real text, so the tool leaves them alone where they do. A complete flag of England, Scotland or Wales (a black flag followed by a few tag letters and a cancel tag), a zero width joiner between two emoji, and an emoji presentation selector after an emoji are all normal. A joiner or non-joiner inside Persian, Hindi or Arabic words is also normal but is reported, because the tool cannot tell the language of your text.

    The cleaned copy removes zero-width, direction, tag, control and other invisible characters. It never changes mixed-script words, because replacing a letter would change the text; fix those by hand after checking the highlighted word.

    Tips

    • Count what is left with the character counter, which counts characters, bytes and lines.
    • Compare the original with the cleaned text in Compare text.
    • To remove one specific character everywhere, use Find and replace.
