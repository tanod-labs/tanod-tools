    What the generated firewall does

    The script follows the same structure as MikroTik's own default configuration, made stricter. The input chain protects the router: it accepts packets belonging to connections already allowed, drops invalid ones, answers a limited rate of pings, lets the management subnets reach WinBox and SSH, accepts the LAN (or only DNS, DHCP, NTP and ICMP from it), and drops everything else with a final rule that has no conditions. A final unconditional drop is what turns a list of exceptions into a default-deny policy.

    The forward chain protects the network behind the router. Established and related traffic is accepted (and optionally fasttracked), invalid packets are dropped, and new connections arriving from the WAN are dropped unless a destination NAT rule (a port forward) created them. That last rule is the one that stops the internet from reaching private addresses behind the router directly, which masquerade alone does not do.

    Services and helpers

    RouterOS listens on several management services by default. The script turns off the plain-text ones (telnet, FTP, WebFig over HTTP) and the API, and adds an address restriction to WinBox and SSH as a second layer behind the firewall. MAC Telnet, MAC WinBox and neighbour discovery work at layer 2 and ignore IP firewall rules, so they are limited to the LAN interface list. The bandwidth test server, web proxy, SOCKS and UPnP are switched off; turn back on only what you use.
